    More of my view on structure and texts¶

    Sett asked for it, so here it is. Marked guesses are guesses.

    • The text marked U may be the most valuable file in the whole source. A statute as published in 1997 is not the statute in force today: it was amended many times, and the amendments sit in other acts. Someone has to work them into one text. From the file names I believe U is that text, kept current by the office of Sejm (guess, to confirm on ISAP). An advisor that answers from the published text of an old statute will be wrong with confidence. It should answer from U where U exists, and say that U is not an official text.
    • Structure makes citation possible. struct gives every article and point an id. A model that is given units with ids can answer "art. 5 ust. 2" and point at it, and a reader can check. A model that is given a wall of text can only paraphrase. For the advisor I would cut texts into articles by these ids before anything is fed anywhere.
    • Three quarters of acts have no HTML and no structure. 39,110 of 164,638 have HTML. The rest is PDF: born digital for newer years, scans for old ones. Before reading scans, count which acts are still in force (inForce in details). I expect most acts in force to be new enough to have a digital PDF or HTML, and most scans to be history (guess; the details will tell once gathered). If so, reading scans is a project for historians and can wait.
    • Status alone is not enough to know what applies. obowiązujący on a statute says the statute stands; it does not say which wording. The graph of references says what amended it and when. Date of the question, status and references together answer "what was the law on that day".
    • Justifications are the missing explanation. A print of Sejm holds the draft and the authors' reasons for it. Processes carry the ELI of the act they ended with, so every newer statute can be joined with the reasons it was written for. That is the closest thing to "why" that exists in public data.
    • Judgments need care before they are published by us. They are public, and they are about people. The source anonymises parties; I would not build anything that searches judgments by a person's name, judges included.

    Ideas that came up while building¶

    • Texts by article. What: one line per article with its id from struct. Where: src/core/parsing/text.ts, table dataset/units/<journal>/<year>.jsonl. How: the HTML marks every unit with id and data-id; walk it once.

    • Text from digital PDF. What: markdown for acts without HTML. Where: src/core/parsing/text.ts, with a field saying how the text was read. How: a PDF library first; reading of scans separately and marked.

    • Join tables. What: process → act, judgment → act. Where: src/core/methods/compile.ts. How: processes carry ELI; judgments carry journalYear and journalEntry in referencedRegulations.

    • Changes of judgments. What: SAOS can list judgments modified since a time (sinceModificationDate). Where: src/core/apihandlers/saos.ts. How: like gather --since, rewriting the months that were touched.

    • Votings and transcripts of Sejm. Transcripts done (phase 35, task sittings); votings still wanted. What: who voted how, and what was said. Where: src/core/apihandlers/sejm.ts. How: the same API, per sitting.

    Next steps inside this source¶

    • Tables from raw files. What: details and references of 164 thousand acts are small JSON files in the bucket; a dataset needs them as tables. Where: src/core/methods/compile.ts, writing into resources/dataset/. How: one JSONL of details per journal and year, and one table of edges from | type | to | art | date built from the references. The edge table is the graph of the law and it will be small enough to open in a notebook.

    • Daily updates in place of full listings. What: API has /changes/acts?since={date} (checked: it answers with acts sorted by change date). Where: a --since option of gather. How: remember the newest changeDate that was seen, ask only for what changed after it, and take the files of those acts again. Without this, an act whose status changes keeps its old details here forever, because a file that exists is never asked for twice.

    • Structure and other texts of an act. What: API also gives /struct (chapters, articles, paragraphs) and more than one PDF per act. Details of DU/2026/1 list four texts of types O, I, T, U. I do not know what the letters mean; my guess is that some of them are consolidated texts. Where: two more entries in PARTS of src/core/apihandlers/eli.ts. How: struct is one more JSON per act. The extra texts need the details first, because their file names are listed there.

    • Text for models. What: HTML exists only for some acts. In the listing of 2026 none of 1321 had it and 1317 had PDF (checked); DU/2024/1907 has 1.4 MB of HTML (checked). Where: a parsing stage after gathering, separate from it. How: prefer HTML where it exists, PDF text where the PDF was born digital, and reading of scans only for the old years, marked as machine-read.

    • Links for people. What: checkup writes what the browser saw on each website that shows an act. Where: resources/dataset/checkups/, shown in the space. How: the space can offer, for every act, the links that were alive at the last visit, with the date of that visit.

    Other government sources in Poland¶

    One request each, made on 2026-10-13, to see whether they answer.

    Source Address What is there State
    Sejm, work of parliament api.sejm.gov.pl/sejm/term{N}/… legislative processes, prints with drafts and justifications, votings, proceedings, interpellations answers; processes carry ELI of the act they ended with
    SAOS, court judgments www.saos.org.pl/api/search/judgments judgments of common courts, Supreme Court, Constitutional Tribunal answers; I did not check who runs it today or how complete it is
    Open data portal api.dane.gov.pl/1.4/datasets catalogue of datasets of public offices answers
    National Court Register api-krs.ms.gov.pl/api/krs/OdpisAktualny/{krs} extract of one registered entity by its number answers; there is no listing, the number has to be known
    Statistics Poland, BDL bdl.stat.gov.pl/api/v1/… local statistics answered 429: limit is 100 requests per 15 minutes
    National Bank api.nbp.pl/api/exchangerates/… exchange rates, gold prices answers
    Government legislation legislacja.rcl.gov.pl drafts before they reach Sejm website only, robots.txt disallows everyone
    Administrative courts orzeczenia.nsa.gov.pl judgments did not answer my request

    What fits this repository, in my order:

    1. Sejm processes and prints. Same host, same manners, same ELI identifiers. It adds how each statute was made: the draft, the justification written by its authors, the votes. For an advisor the justification is often the clearest explanation of what an article is for.
    2. Judgments. The law as it is applied. They cite acts by the same addresses, so they attach to the graph of references.
    3. The rest is data about the country and not law. It belongs in another repository, if anywhere.

    European Union law is a border case. Polish acts name the directives they carry out (field directives in details), and EUR-Lex has its own open access. It is not a foreign government, and it is law in force in Poland. I would decide that after the Polish graph exists.

    Moving data to huggingface¶

    • Skip what is already there. What: on a second machine the gatherer would ask Sejm again for files that the bucket already holds. Where: src/core/methods/gather.ts, next to the check of local files. How: read the listing of the bucket once at start and treat those paths as gathered.

    • Buckets are compared by size only. What: a bucket does not give a git hash, so src/core/resources/hub.ts calls a file the same when path and size agree. How to do better: keep our own list of hashes in the dataset, written at gathering time.

    • One number worth knowing before the big run. A dataset repository of huggingface is a git repository, and git is unhappy with hundreds of thousands of files. That is why raw files of acts go to the bucket and only tables go to the dataset.
